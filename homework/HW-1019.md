## Q6 · By hand — a logistic model of passing

The exercise gives a fitted logistic regression of "receives an A" on hours studied and GPA, with
coefficients −6, 0.05 and 1.

**(a)** Compute the probability for a student with 40 hours and GPA 3.5. Show the log-odds, then
the probability. Work it by hand; a photo of your working is fine.

**(b)** How many hours does that student need for a 50% chance? Show the equation you solved.

**(c) Not in the book.** Repeat (a) for 50 hours. By how many percentage points did the
probability rise? Compare with the coefficient 0.05 × 10 = 0.5 and explain, in two sentences,
why they are not the same number. *Most of the credit for Q6 is here.*

## Q9 · By hand — odds and probabilities

**(a)** Odds of 0.37: what fraction default? **(b)** 16% default: what are the odds?
Then **(c) not in the book**: a lender says "the odds doubled, so the probability doubled".
For a starting probability of 0.16 and for 0.60, compute what doubling the odds does to the
probability. One sentence on when the lender's sentence is approximately right.

## Q12 · Conceptual — two ways to code a multinomial model

The exercise compares softmax coding over K classes with coding against a baseline class.

**(a)–(c)** Follow the book: write the two forms, and show how the coefficients of one map to the
coefficients of the other.

**(d)** In one paragraph: if two analysts fit the same multinomial model with the two codings and
report different coefficient tables, is either of them wrong? What is the same in both tables?
