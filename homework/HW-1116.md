## Q8 · Cross-validation on simulated data

**(a)** Generate the data as the book says. State n, p, and write the true model as an equation.

**(b)** Scatterplot of X against Y. One sentence on what you see.

**(c)** LOOCV error for the four polynomial models (degrees 1–4), with the seed the book gives.

**(d)** Repeat with a different seed. Are the four errors the same? Explain why in one sentence —
the answer is a property of LOOCV, not of your code.

**(e)** Which degree has the smallest LOOCV error? Is that what you expected from the equation
in (a)? Say why.

**(f)** The p-values of the coefficients in the degree-4 fit. Do they agree with the
cross-validation result? **One paragraph** on what each of the two tools is telling you and
why they can be read together. *Most of the credit for Q8 is here.*

## Q9 · The bootstrap on house prices (`Boston`, `medv`)

**(a)–(b)** The sample mean and its standard error from the formula s/√n.

**(c)** The bootstrap standard error of the mean (1,000 resamples). Compare with (b) in one sentence.

**(d)** A 95% confidence interval from the bootstrap (mean ± 2 SE), and the one from a t-test.
How different are they?

**(e)–(f)** The median and its bootstrap SE. The formula in (b) does not apply here: say why the
bootstrap was needed.

**(g)–(h)** The 10th percentile and its bootstrap SE. Then **one paragraph**: a colleague reports
"the 10th percentile of house value is 12.75" with no standard error. Using your numbers, say how
many digits of that figure mean anything.
