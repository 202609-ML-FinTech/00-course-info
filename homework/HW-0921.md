## Q2 · Hierarchical clustering by hand

The exercise gives a **dissimilarity matrix for four observations**. Work from that matrix
directly — there is no data set and no code to write.

**(a) Complete linkage.** Sketch the dendrogram. Mark **the height of every fusion** and label
each leaf with its observation number.

**(b) Single linkage.** Repeat (a).

**(c)** Cut your dendrogram from (a) so that two clusters result. Which observations are in each?

**(d)** Cut your dendrogram from (b) so that two clusters result. Which observations are in each?

**(e)** Redraw the dendrogram from (a) with two or more leaves repositioned, so that it looks
different but means exactly the same thing. Say in one sentence why the two drawings are
equivalent.

**Draw (a), (b) and (e) by hand and upload photographs.** A figure produced by `scipy` earns no
marks for this question — the whole exercise is whether *you* can carry out the linkage.

For each fusion in (a) and (b), show the dissimilarity you used and where it came from. The
difference between complete and single linkage lives in exactly that choice, and parts (c) and
(d) are there to make you notice that the two can disagree.

## Q3 · K-means by hand

Six observations, two features, **K = 2**. Follow the book's parts:

**(a)** Plot the six observations.
**(b)** Assign each one a random cluster label, and report the labels you got.
**(c)** Compute the centroid of each cluster.
**(d)** Reassign every observation to its nearest centroid, by Euclidean distance.
**(e)** Repeat (c) and (d) until nothing changes.
**(f)** Plot the observations again, coloured by their final cluster.

**Show every iteration** — the centroids and the full set of labels at each step, in a table.
An answer that states only the final clustering earns little credit, because the final answer
is the part an AI can produce without doing anything.

Then answer two questions that are **not** in the book:

**(g)** How many iterations did your run take? Start again from a *different* random assignment
in (b) and run it to convergence a second time. Report both runs.

**(h)** Did the two runs reach the same final clusters? Say what that tells you about the
`n_init` argument you have been passing to `KMeans` without thinking about it.
